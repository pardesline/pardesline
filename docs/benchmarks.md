# Benchmarks

Package defaults against upstream defaults, same inputs, same metrics, numbers generated by the scripts in [`benchmarks/`](https://github.com/pardesline/pardesline/tree/main/benchmarks) and committed with the command that produced them. Nothing in this page is typed by hand. Run `python benchmarks/run_all.py` to regenerate `benchmarks/results.md`; the raw per-trial numbers are in `benchmarks/results/*.json`.

What is compared and why it is fair:

- **Registration**: the moving cloud is a rotated (35°), partial (70 % overlap) and noisy (0.5 % of the diagonal) copy of the target, a different noise draw per trial. Open3D's ICP is called the way its tutorial calls it (identity start, point-to-point, threshold 0.02); Open3D's global registration uses the tutorial's `voxel_size = 0.05`; pardesline uses `target.align(moving)` with the catalog defaults. The rotation error is measured against the known transform.
- **Reconstruction**: a noisy partial scan with a hole; Open3D's `create_from_point_cloud_poisson(depth=8)` versus `points.to_mesh()` (depth 9, density-trimmed); Chamfer distance to the original mesh and the fraction of reconstructed surface farther than 2 mm from it (the balloon over the hole).

The upstream calls are the documented, tutorial-level calls, which is what a practitioner copies first. An expert can of course tune Open3D to the same result: the point of the package is that the tuning is written down and applied by default.

## Reading the numbers

- **Registration**: the tutorial ICP call stays near its 32° start (a local optimiser from a wrong pose); the tutorial RANSAC call fails worse because its fixed `voxel_size = 0.05` is a third of the bunny, so there are almost no features to match. The package's defaults derive the voxel from the bounding box and land at 0.11° median, 0.24° worst over five noise draws, at the cost of 5 s instead of 0.2 s.
- **Reconstruction**: this one is a trade-off, not a win on every metric. The density-trimmed surface has four times less area far from the truth (0.8 % vs 3.4 %: the balloon over the hole is gone) but a higher Chamfer distance (1.14 mm vs 0.80 mm), because the symmetric Chamfer also measures truth-to-reconstruction, and a trimmed hole is "missing surface" where the untrimmed one offers a wrong but nearby guess. Which is better depends on what you do next: for metrology and printing, no invented surface; for rendering, the closed guess may look better. Both settings are one argument away (`density_quantile`).

## Limits

- The bunny is a public stand-in for industrial scans; the characteristics (partial overlap, noise, unknown pose) are representative, the material is not.
- RANSAC is randomised: expect a few hundredths of a degree of spread between runs on the registration median, more on the worst case.
- No benchmark yet for volume registration or segmentation defaults: the RIRE and MSD examples show single runs with their numbers, and the [benchmarks tracking issue](https://github.com/pardesline/pardesline/issues?q=is%3Aissue+label%3Aroadmap+benchmarks) lists what comes next.
